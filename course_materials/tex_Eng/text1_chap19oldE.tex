The most commonly used practice in psychology is the test for differences in means. As we learned in experimental design, it's used in conjunction with RCTs to understand the size of the effect as the difference in group means that are observed.
In this lecture, we will first consider testing the difference in means between two groups. In the case of paired two groups, it actually becomes a judgment on one "distribution of differences", so we can have a similar discussion as before.

\section{One Factor Within-Subjects Design, 2 Levels}
Up until now, we have explained the concept of the null hypothesis test and its formal procedure, and discussed specific ways to report results and how to interpret them, using examples such as the testing of mean values and correlation coefficients.

In this article, we will be discussing testing the difference in means between two groups, which is a common practice in psychological research.
As mentioned in Chapter \ref{chapter11}, the basic framework of experiments is to "see the difference between the intervened group and the non-intervened group in terms of the difference in group means". It is a model to examine whether the "difference" in the average values of two groups, the experimental group and the control group, is significant.

In terms of experimental design terminology, this can be described as a one-factor, mixed-factor, two-level design. For those who are unsure about the terms factor, between-group/within-group, and level, please refer back to the materials in Chapter 12 for confirmation.

In this section, we will first discuss experimental designs with a single factor and within-group (also known as repeated measures), in particular, the design with two levels. This experimental design only has two levels of measurement within the group, meaning it is an experiment to observe whether there was an effect before and after the intervention. To be specific, within-group designs not only include repeated measures but also include cases where there is correspondence between the data. For example, in a twin study or a study of two individuals in an intimate relationship (which is generally referred to as "intimate relationships" in social psychology and is a good expression that is not limited to gender), as there is a correspondence relationship between the pairs and there is a high possibility of mutual influence, it is thought that there is a correlation between the two variables. In such situations, it is necessary to conduct an analysis that takes into account the underlying factors that they share. Within-group designs are also referred to as "analyses with correspondence."

Let's assume this is a study that took pre and post data. For example, regarding psychological health status, a questionnaire survey was conducted in advance, and then some kind of clinical intervention was performed (such as relaxation technique training), and after a certain period of time, a post-survey was conducted on the same person. This is the research design, as shown in Figure \ref{fig::19_01}.

\input{text1_chap19old_escape_01}

Assuming that the subjects gathered in advance were collected randomly from the population and that the health status scores determined by the preliminary survey followed a normal distribution, if the same test is conducted afterwards and there is an overall effect despite individual differences, it can be said that this clinical intervention is excellent. While individual client changes are important, it is natural to want to verify changes in the population and differences in the population mean more generally by borrowing the power of inferential statistics. Therefore, by estimating the population mean from the sample mean and assuming that the population distribution is normal, the width of the sample mean fluctuation can also be assumed. This is used to estimate the likely location of the population mean and consider how it differs between before and after. Also, since we do not know the population mean or standard deviation, we consider the unbiased variance calculated from the sample as an estimate. When estimating from sample statistics, it follows a t-distribution, not a normal distribution, so we also use a t-distribution for estimation. We consider testing using this estimated value, and this test is called a t-test, which is a commonly used pattern because it uses a t-distribution for estimation and testing. By the way, the t in t-test comes from the fact that the test uses a t-distribution. There are actually many other estimations and tests that use a t-distribution besides the mean difference test, but this is a widely used pattern, so the two-sample mean difference test is called a t-test. By the way, please note that the t in t-test is lowercase. The uppercase T is used for other tests and can be confusing.


Now, we want to know if there has been any change before and after a certain event. Fortunately, we know that the difference between the normally distributed random variable X and the normally distributed random variable Y, X-Y, is also normally distributed. Since there is corresponding data available, we can use this feature to determine if there has been any change before and after the event.

Let's consider this in terms of the logic of examination.
\paragraph{Setting the hypothesis}
In order to conduct the research, it is necessary to establish both a null hypothesis and an alternative hypothesis. The null hypothesis indicates that there is no difference between the before and after conditions, and there is no effect. If we express this using symbols, it would be $\mu_D=0$. On the other hand, the alternative hypothesis indicates that there is a clinical effect, which means that the difference in the dependent variable should be greater than 0. In other words, it would be $\mu_D > 0$.
\paragraph{Select Test Statistic} As we are estimating the mean value, the appropriate test statistic would be $t$.
"Set the probability that becomes the basis for judgment." Let's decide the outcome with a significance level alpha of 5%, as we did this time."
\paragraph{Calculate the realized value of the test statistic} As we have conducted interval estimation earlier, we did not calculate $\frac{\bar{D}-\mu_D}{\hat{\sigma_D}/\sqrt{n}}$. Let's do that now. \underline{If we follow the null hypothesis, $\mu_D=0$}, so...
\[t= \frac{\bar{D}-\mu_D}{\hat{\sigma_D}/\sqrt{n}}  = \frac{2.4-0}{3.062/\sqrt{10}}  = 2.479\]
This $t=2.479$ follows a t-distribution with 9 degrees of freedom. The probability of getting a t-value greater than or equal to this observed value is $p=0.0175$.
"Judging whether the initially defined hypothesis is correct or incorrect."
The calculated value of this t-test under the null hypothesis is smaller than the 5% significance level. Therefore, we can say that it is unusual. Since something unusual has occurred, it is possible that our assumptions are incorrect. In other words, it is strange to assume that there is no difference. We will reject the null hypothesis and accept the alternative hypothesis.

"Let's Settle It with a Test, Shall We?" (literal translation) or "Let's Decide the Outcome with a Test" (idiomatic translation)
Here comes the role of hypothesis testing. Similar to the previous example, we use the characteristic that "the difference between two normal distributions is also a normal distribution" to perform the test. Assuming a random sample is taken from a normal distribution population $N(\mu, \sigma)$, the sample mean $\bar{X}$ follows $N(\mu, \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}})$. Now, considering the difference between the sample means of two groups, $\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2$, we know that the distribution follows a mean of $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ and a standard deviation of $\sigma/\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}$. However, since we do not know the value of $\sigma$, we need to replace it with an unbiased estimator. In this case, since there are two groups, using the unbiased variance of either the experimental group or the control group alone would be problematic. Thus, we need to combine the two groups and use the pooled unbiased estimator of variance.
\[ \hat{\sigma^2}_{pooled} = \frac{(n_1-1)\hat{\sigma_1^2}+(n_2-1)\hat{\sigma_2^2}}{n_1+n_2 - 2}\]
We use "pooled", which means amassed or combined. Since this is an estimate of the variance, the positive square root of this serves as an estimate of the standard deviation of the difference in distribution.
You can use the following formula: \[\hat{\sigma}_{pooled} = \sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)\hat{\sigma_1^2}+(n_2-1)\hat{\sigma_2^2}}{n_1+n_2 - 2}}\]

It's getting a bit complicated, but using this, we can calculate the statistic as follows.

\[ t = \frac{\bar{X_1}-\bar{X_2}-(\mu_1-\mu_2)}{\sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)\hat{\sigma_1^2}+(n_2-1)\hat{\sigma_2^2}}{n_1+n_2 - 2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}}\]

Next, it was determined that it can be completely calculated from the sample statistics. Let us proceed with the testing.
\paragraph{Setting the hypothesis} Both the null hypothesis and the alternative hypothesis are necessary. As mentioned earlier, the null hypothesis is that $\mu_1=\mu_2$, and the alternative hypothesis is the negation of this statement, which is $\mu_1 \neq \mu_2$.
\paragraph{Selecting the test statistic} As we are estimating the mean value, the test statistic we should use is $t$.
"Set the probability that becomes the criteria for judgement. Let's decide with a significance level of alpha, which in this case is 5%."
\paragraph{Calculating the realized value of the test statistic} This is a bit tricky, but following the formula, let's denote the control group as $X_1$ and the experimental group as $X_2$. Then, we have $\bar{X_1}=20.60, \hat{\sigma}_1=4.393, \bar{X_2}=25.20, \hat{\sigma}_2=6.221$.

\[ t = \frac{20.60-25.20}{\sqrt{(4.393^2+6.221^2)/5}} = -1.3506\]

Since this has become $(n_1-1)+(n_2-1)=n_1+n_2-2=10-2=8$ degrees of freedom, it follows a $t$ distribution with degrees of freedom of 8. From this, the probability of obtaining a more extreme $t$ value is $p=0.1068".

In reality, we do not know the shape of the population. Especially when it comes to psychological responses, we cannot truly know how scores are distributed in the population. However, we can assume that it has a normal distribution. We then perform some operations, but we cannot know the actual effect of these operations. Before conducting research, we must carefully consider this. Even if we can change a person's mental state through some manipulation, if the change is too small, it may not be detected. Similarly, even if the change is large (such as increasing the difference in average values), if there is a lot of individual variation, it may not be detected.

As the average increases and the standard deviation decreases, the degree of detection increases. Therefore, place the difference in means in the numerator and the standard deviation in the denominator.

\[ EffectSize =  \frac{\mu_1-\mu_2}{\sigma} \]

We call the result of this, which we created by standardizing the difference, the \keytermE{effect size}. Essentially, it represents the extent to which it is easy to detect a significant difference. However, it is important to repeat that the goal is not to try to achieve a significant difference. Rather, even for excellent research themes, we simply want to convey the fact that it is highly likely that we cannot accurately judge the results unless they are done with large effect sizes. Psychological research involves studying things that are invisible and delicate. Additionally, the quality of the data may be compromised due to errors that can easily creep in when data is collected about the complexities of the mind. Therefore, we proceed with the research while hypothesizing whether the effect is big or small based on previous studies. As you become more interested in various psychological phenomena, this will become clearer in different specialized areas. Regardless, it is important to keep in mind that when using statistical tests, it is possible to encounter situations where "the effect cannot be detected as well as we expected."
