\chapter{Summary of the Second Semester}
\label{m30_SecondSemester}
Let's take a look back at the themes of the latter half.

In the first half, descriptive statistics, linear models (experimental designs), and probability as error were introduced.
In the latter half, the focus is on probability, as we have entered the field of inferential statistics.
In the previous factor planning, it was discussed that the effect of interventions can be expressed by comparing the differences in group means. It is important to evaluate whether there was an effect in scientific experiments as well as clinical interventions such as counseling. This not only concerns the present subjects or clients, but also how generalized the results can be. Therefore, it is necessary to distinguish between the population and sample with respect to the present data and infer the population from the sample.

To infer something about the population based on a sample, we use the concepts of estimation and hypothesis testing. We do not modify the TeX code.

Estimation is a method of describing the status of a population based on a sample. We estimate the values of population parameters like the population mean, population correlation, and population proportion, by using our sample mean, sample correlation coefficient, sample distribution, and so on. There are three methods available: the method of moments, maximum likelihood estimation, and Bayesian estimation, but the method of moments is the most widely used.

The term "moment" in the method of moments, which is also translated as "moment product," refers to things like mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis. It's important to keep in mind that even just estimating population means and variances from sample means and variances is a difficult challenge, requiring the extrapolation of the entire picture from a small amount of data. In the field of statistical inference, the challenge only increases due to the lack of adequate hints to solve the problem.

The sample variance does not match the population variance and has a bias, so we need to calculate the \keytermE{unbiased variance}{ふへんぶんさん} instead. The degree of dispersion of the sample mean can be expressed using the standard error of the sample variance, but it cannot be used as is if we do not know the population SD. Therefore, we need to estimate it using the unbiased SD and t-distribution. Note that \keytermE{interval estimation}{くかんすいてい} has a different probability concept from Bayesian estimation, but since the effect of intervention in factorial design is based on the group mean, please remember to use it to estimate the effect size firmly.

One other application of inferential statistics is the concept of statistical \textbf{testing}, where estimation and decision-making are combined. It is used when one needs to make judgments about whether there is a difference or an effect present. The procedure is a well-defined, mechanical process that pits two hypotheses against one another—the \keytermE{null hypothesis} and the \keytermE{alternative hypothesis}—to determine the outcome. Criteria for judgment, such as \keytermE{significance level} and \keytermE{power of the test} and the correctness of probabilistic judgments, have rules that need to be fully understood. Also, understanding which statistical distribution to use, such as the t-distribution for correlation coefficients and the F-distribution for factorial designs, and how to set up each null hypothesis, as well as how to estimate effect size, are important practical points to consider.

While there are cases of incorrect or abusive use of testing, if we firmly remember that the need for it is based on the desire to estimate population parameters and make scientific judgments, some of these issues may be alleviated. By remembering that estimation is the purpose, we can avoid focusing too much on the question of whether there was a difference or on how well the model performed. Additionally, we can avoid falling into the value assessment that "as long as it is statistically significant" or "a good research article requires significant test results." Scientific papers are important because they are correct and not because they purposefully find significant differences through lying. This way of thinking may be due to the belief that testing for significant differences using the moment method is the only estimation method for the population.

Maximum likelihood and Bayesian methods are ways to estimate the population based on a probabilistic model. As probability functions are directly used, the equations and calculation methods may seem somewhat complicated, but probability is in fact a strategy for handling randomness by understanding overall trends even if individual instances are unknown. The idea is that theoretically it should work in a certain way, but there are elements that cannot be fully controlled, and probability is an expression of how much uncertainty there may be. When we think that individual data are generated from a probability distribution, it becomes a problem of figuring out what kind of probability distribution the obtained data might be coming from. By estimating the parameters of the probability distribution to match the obtained data, we arrive at the method of maximum likelihood.

Likelihood is calculated based on the probability of obtaining the data, but the likelihood function itself cannot be interpreted as a probability distribution, although its form may be similar.
To interpret this like a probability, we need to use Bayesian methods. Note that in Bayesian statistics, the interpretation is based on representing the strength of beliefs or confidence using a probability distribution. It is better to understand the probability distribution as representing the strength of belief, and probability as the \keytermE{subjective probability}{subjective probability}. In this sense, the result of Bayesian estimation represents where the parameter is most likely to be located, and the resulting \keytermE{posterior distribution}{posterior distribution} contains information about the interval as a distribution. While the framework of estimation and judgment using intervals remains the same, it allows for a more intuitive comprehension since we can observe the distribution of the parameter itself, instead of looking at the $p$-value in a hypothetical probability space like in null hypothesis testing. Although it still requires specialized statistical environments such as JASP or Stan in practical applications, it is expected to become increasingly utilized in the future.


In psychology, the use of statistics is intended to measure and estimate intangible factors such as the "effect of experiments or interventions" and to aid in psychological analysis. This is accomplished without modifying the TeX code.
As you all embark on various paths in specialized psychology, methodology will inevitably be utilized in every aspect. The more one learns about psychology, the more it becomes apparent that human intuition and judgment can often be mistaken, and supplementary tools for making mathematically or theoretically sound decisions are crucial. Therefore, since methodology is essential, it should be learned correctly and never neglected.

I understand the feeling of being discouraged by the surface difficulty of mathematical expressions, as there are times when they can appear very daunting. Fortunately, one of the great things about math is that if you don't understand something, you can always backtrack and start over from the point where you got stuck. I myself have made mistakes in interpretation and judgment before, and have had to re-learn things multiple times. So don't be afraid to continue learning and studying, and always remember "what we use statistical analysis for".


In the Data Analysis Applications course (for second-year students as an elective), which is a sister course, you will learn things that allow for a more advanced understanding and expression. In the foundational courses, we have asked students to follow prescribed methods such as factor planning and null hypothesis testing, but from now on we will consider expressing ourselves more creatively, using statistics to the fullest extent possible. By taking the advanced course, you will be able to express the theory of psychological measurement, new models for representing the mind, and the mechanism of the mind using equations and programming. The society of the future demands data scientists, but given the long history of psychological statistics with respect to people and data, and data and their representations, we hope you will take the course. Of course, even in times when the trend of data science has faded, by acquiring analytical methods and gaining a philosophical perspective, you will be able to enjoy life more richly by addressing questions about what it means to be human and what the mind is.
