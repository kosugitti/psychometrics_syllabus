\chapter{Summary of the First Semester}
\label{chapter15}
We have reached the end of the first half of our discussion. Let's take a look back at what we've discussed so far.

In the first half of the class, the main topics were "description" and "causation and correlation".

At the beginning, we talked about why mathematics is necessary in psychology. Many students who choose to pursue humanities think that they can avoid mathematics because it is not a required subject, especially when dealing with the wet and literary subject of the human mind. However, nowadays, mathematical approaches are essential in not only literature, but also law, history, and other fields, as every science is based on objective thinking informed by data. Mathematical thinking may seem dull and uninteresting, but precisely because it is plain and emotionless without subjective color, it is necessary for scientific thinking. Recently, the demand for data scientists has increased, and the purpose behind the data we deal with is to understand the "mind" behind human reactions. Knowing this is much more socially necessary than merely considering data as mere numbers without any insight. Having mathematical thinking while studying psychology and dealing with data will make individuals very attractively positioned for society in the future, so please have confidence in your abilities.

You may have already learned the basics of descriptive statistics under the recent guidelines for school education. Although learning how to create histograms, line graphs, box and whisker plots, and scatter plots might have seemed like an unexciting subject, if you consider them as expressions of human responses and manifestations of the mind, you should not see numbers as mere meaningless series. Moreover, if you realize that by collecting a lot of data, you can discover some kind of trend (e.g., normal distribution or correlation) that you could not see in each particular data point, you can also acquire a panoramic perspective that prevents you from being caught up in daily examples in front of you. Isn't it exciting?

The methods of descriptive statistics and visualization, which used to require skilled craftsmanship, can now be easily expressed using computer technology. You may think that the R language is difficult, but it is necessary to maintain objectivity and procedural legitimacy by mechanically returning the expected response in machine language. As a university student in the field of science, you must also learn these protocols. Although it may be difficult at first, please make an effort to fully utilize the computer as a writing tool.

During the course, we discussed measurement (Chapter \ref{m06_ctt}) and probability (Chapter \ref{m07_probability}). Afterwards, we delved into content that was closer to the practice of psychological research, including correlation, causation, and experimental design. Studying something that cannot be seen is already a difficult discipline, but this is why we must think while collecting data and utilize the power of statistics and probability. You may think you understand because you believe you have your own mind, but it is actually the opposite. We tend to deceive ourselves with our own thoughts, believe that our mind is the cause, and become convinced of experiment results that seem plausible. We must penetrate this "self-deception" of the mind and use data to objectively find the truth. There are also fluctuations in research subjects, such as individual differences and random errors that are not present in objects, but we must learn to control them through probability and experimental design.

Have you come to realize that psychology is a more complicated field than you originally thought? In other words, it's not as simple as just thinking for a moment and understanding something as complex as the human mind. To truly free yourself from the tendencies to deceive yourself with thoughts like "this is just how I am" or "this is how society is," you need to build a solid foundation of knowledge and skills in this area.

As we enter the latter half, this story becomes even more complex.
Up to this point, we have been striving to understand the meaning of the data in front of us and to uncover the information contained within it.
As you enter the later stages, you start thinking about generalizing beyond the data in front of you. While your current data may look like this, what about the next data and future data? Your current data may only come from people close to you, but what about the society and the world at large? You need to expand your thinking further, and that's where probability and statistics come into play once again.

Please look forward to it.
