\begin{itemize}
	\item A must precede B (temporal precedence).
	\item The variable identified as the cause is not the cause of many other variables. In other words, it is not like $A \to B, A \to C, A \to D \cdots$ (e.g. it cannot be said that someone committed a crime because they had breakfast).
	\item There is no common cause Z that affects both A and B, i.e. $Z \to A, Z \to B$ (e.g. the common cause of parents liking violent images and raising troublesome children).
\end{itemize}
